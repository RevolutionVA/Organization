## Article I. Purpose and Scope

**Section 1.1. Purpose.** RevolutionVA (the "Corporation") expects its directors, officers and
volunteers to act honestly and lawfully and to protect the Corporation's charitable assets. This
Policy (a) encourages anyone with a good-faith concern about wrongdoing to raise it, (b) sets out
how reports are made, received and investigated, and (c) protects people who report from
retaliation.

**Section 1.2. Who May Report.** Any director, officer, committee member, volunteer, contractor,
speaker, sponsor, vendor, attendee or member of the public (each a "Reporter") may make a report
under this Policy. If the Corporation ever has employees, they are covered too.

**Section 1.3. What to Report.** Reports under this Policy concern suspected:

- (a) fraud, theft, embezzlement or misuse of the Corporation's funds or property, including sponsorship, ticket or donation revenue;
- (b) false or misleading financial records, reports, Form 990 filings or donor acknowledgments;
- (c) violations of federal, state or local law, including tax law and charitable solicitation rules;
- (d) violations of the Corporation's Conflict of Interest Policy, its Bylaws or other Board-adopted policies;
- (e) use of the Corporation for private benefit or inurement, or any action that could jeopardize its tax-exempt status (Bylaws § 4.2);
- (f) destruction or concealment of records in violation of the Document Retention and Destruction Policy or the law; and
- (g) retaliation against anyone for making a report under this Policy.

**Section 1.4. What This Policy Does Not Cover.** Harassment, safety and conduct concerns at a
RevolutionVA event or in a RevolutionVA online space should first go through the applicable event
Code of Conduct, which has its own reporting process. A Code of Conduct matter becomes a matter
under this Policy if it involves an item in Section 1.3, or if someone is retaliated against for
reporting it. Ordinary disagreements about programming, event decisions or strategy are not
whistleblower matters and should be raised with the Board directly.

## Article II. Definitions

**Section 2.1. Compliance Officer.** The **Secretary** serves as Compliance Officer under this
Policy. If the Secretary is the subject of a report, is otherwise involved, or is unavailable, the
Board shall designate another director who is not involved to act as Compliance Officer for that
report.

**Section 2.2. Disinterested Director.** A director who is not the subject of the report, is not
alleged to have participated in the conduct reported, and has no Financial Interest (as defined in
the Conflict of Interest Policy) in the outcome.

**Section 2.3. Good Faith.** A Reporter acts in good faith when the Reporter has a reasonable basis
for believing the information reported is true. A report made in good faith is protected even if
it turns out to be mistaken.

## Article III. How to Report

**Section 3.1. Reporting Channels.** A Reporter may make a report, orally or in writing (including
by email), to any of the following:

- (a) the **President**;
- (b) the **Compliance Officer** (the Secretary); or
- (c) any other director.

The Corporation shall publish current contact details for these roles at
_[board contact address or page, to be confirmed on adoption]_.

**Section 3.2. Alternate Channel When the President Is Involved.** If a report concerns the
President, the Reporter should not report to the President. The Reporter should report to the
Compliance Officer or to any Disinterested Director, who shall handle the report without the
President's participation and shall not disclose it to the President except as needed to give the
President a fair opportunity to respond under Section 5.3.

**Section 3.3. When Every Director Is Involved.** The Board is permitted to have as few as one
director (Bylaws § 2.1). If a report concerns every director, or the Reporter reasonably believes
no director can handle it impartially, the Reporter may report directly to the Corporation's
outside legal counsel or independent accountant. That advisor may recommend how the matter should
be handled, including the election of an additional independent director under Bylaws § 2.2.

**Section 3.4. Anonymous Reports.** Reports may be made anonymously. Anonymous reports will be
reviewed, but the Corporation may be unable to investigate fully or to follow up without a way to
contact the Reporter. Reporters are encouraged to provide enough detail to allow an investigation.

**Section 3.5. Outside Reporting.** Nothing in this Policy prevents anyone from reporting a concern
directly to a government agency, such as the Internal Revenue Service (Form 13909), the Virginia
Office of the Attorney General, or law enforcement, or requires anyone to report internally first.

**Section 3.6. Duty of Directors and Officers.** A director or officer who receives a report, or
who becomes aware of conduct described in Section 1.3, shall promptly forward it to the Compliance
Officer, or to a Disinterested Director when the Compliance Officer is involved.

## Article IV. Protections

**Section 4.1. Good Faith.** Anyone making a report must act in good faith. A person who knowingly
makes a false report, or makes a report for the purpose of harming another person, is not
protected by this Policy and may be subject to corrective action, including removal from a
volunteer role, office or the Board (Bylaws §§ 2.2, 10.2).

**Section 4.2. Confidentiality.** The identity of the Reporter and the contents of a report shall
be kept confidential to the extent possible, consistent with the need to conduct a fair
investigation, take corrective action, and comply with law. Information shall be shared only with
those who need it to handle the matter.

**Section 4.3. No Retaliation.** No director, officer, volunteer or other person acting for the
Corporation shall retaliate against anyone for making a good-faith report, for cooperating in an
investigation, or for refusing to take part in conduct the person reasonably believes is unlawful.
Retaliation includes removal from or reduction of a volunteer role, speaking slot, sponsorship or
event participation, exclusion from events, harassment, threats, and any other adverse action
taken because of a report. Federal law, 18 U.S.C. § 1513(e), makes it a crime to retaliate against
a person for providing truthful information to law enforcement about a possible federal offense,
and that law applies to every organization whether or not this Policy exists.

**Section 4.4. Reporting Retaliation.** Anyone who believes they have been retaliated against
should report it through any channel in Article III. Retaliation is itself a violation of this
Policy, and anyone found to have retaliated is subject to corrective action, including removal
from office or the Board.

## Article V. Handling and Investigation

**Section 5.1. Acknowledgment.** The person receiving a report shall forward it to the Compliance
Officer (or under Section 3.2 or 3.3 when applicable). The Compliance Officer shall acknowledge
receipt to an identified Reporter within ten (10) business days.

**Section 5.2. Assessment.** The Compliance Officer, with the Disinterested Directors, shall decide
whether the report falls under this Policy and whether it warrants investigation. Matters that
belong under a Code of Conduct or another process shall be referred there, and the Reporter told
so where possible.

**Section 5.3. Investigation.** Investigations shall be prompt, fair and proportionate to the
seriousness of the matter. The Disinterested Directors may conduct the investigation themselves or
engage outside counsel, an independent accountant or another qualified advisor (Bylaws § 4.1). A
person who is the subject of a report shall be told of the substance of the allegations, at a time
that will not compromise the investigation, and given a fair opportunity to respond. No person who
is the subject of a report, or involved in the conduct reported, shall take part in the
investigation or in the Board's decision on it.

**Section 5.4. Preserving Records.** When a report is received, the Compliance Officer shall
consider whether a litigation hold is needed under the Document Retention and Destruction Policy,
and no one shall destroy or alter records related to the matter while it is open.

**Section 5.5. Outcome.** The Disinterested Directors shall decide on any corrective action, which
may include repayment, changes to financial controls, removal from a role, office or the Board, or
referral to the appropriate authorities. An identified Reporter shall be told when the matter is
closed and, where appropriate and consistent with confidentiality, the general outcome.

## Article VI. Records and Reporting to the Board

**Section 6.1. Log.** The Compliance Officer shall keep a confidential log of each report showing
the date received, a summary of the concern, the steps taken and the outcome. The log and
investigation files are not published.

**Section 6.2. Retention.** Reports, the log and investigation files shall be kept for at least
seven (7) years after the matter is closed, or longer if required by the Document Retention and
Destruction Policy or a litigation hold.

**Section 6.3. Annual Summary.** At each annual meeting of the Board (Bylaws § 3.1), the Compliance
Officer shall report the number and general nature of reports received and how they were resolved,
without identifying Reporters. The minutes shall record that the summary was given.

## Article VII. Distribution and Acknowledgment

**Section 7.1. Distribution.** This Policy shall be published in this repository and linked from
the Corporation's website, and a copy shall be provided to each director, officer and committee
member, and to lead volunteers for each RevolutionVA program, upon taking that role.

**Section 7.2. Annual Acknowledgment.** Each director and officer shall sign the statement in
[Appendix A](#appendix-a-whistleblower-policy-acknowledgment) within 30 days after this Policy is
adopted, upon election, and at or before each annual meeting of the Board. The Conflict of Interest
annual statement may be collected at the same time. Electronic signatures are acceptable. Signed
statements are kept with the Corporation's records by the Secretary and are not published.

## Article VIII. Amendment and Review

This Policy may be amended only by the Board. The Board shall review it at least every two years,
whenever the Bylaws are restated, and whenever the size of the Board changes in a way that affects
the channels in Article III.
