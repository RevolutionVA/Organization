## Article I — Purpose

**Section 1.1. Purpose.** The purpose of this Policy is to protect the interests of RevolutionVA
(the "Corporation") when it is contemplating entering into a transaction or arrangement that might
benefit the private interest of an officer or director of the Corporation, or might result in an
excess benefit transaction (Bylaws Article XIII).

**Section 1.2. Relationship to Law and the Bylaws.** This Policy supplements, and does not replace,
any applicable state or federal law governing conflicts of interest applicable to nonprofit and
charitable organizations, including the Virginia Nonstock Corporation Act and Section 4958 of the
Internal Revenue Code. It is the "Conflict of Interest Policy" referred to in Bylaws §§ 5.1
(transactions with the Corporation), 5.2 (director compensation), 7.2(a) (amendments reserved to the
Board), 11.6 (officer compensation) and 14.1(iii) (indemnification).

## Article II — Definitions

**Section 2.1. Interested Person.** Any director, principal officer, or member of a committee with
Board-delegated powers who has a direct or indirect Financial Interest, as defined below, is an
Interested Person.

**Section 2.2. Financial Interest.** A person has a Financial Interest if the person has, directly
or indirectly, through business, investment, or a Family Member:

- (a) an ownership or investment interest in any entity with which the Corporation has a transaction or arrangement;
- (b) a compensation arrangement with the Corporation or with any entity or individual with which the Corporation has a transaction or arrangement; or
- (c) a potential ownership or investment interest in, or compensation arrangement with, any entity or individual with which the Corporation is negotiating a transaction or arrangement.

Compensation includes direct and indirect remuneration as well as gifts or favors that are not
insubstantial. A Financial Interest is not necessarily a conflict of interest. Under Section 3.2, a
person who has a Financial Interest may have a conflict of interest only if the Board decides that a
conflict of interest exists.

**Section 2.3. Family Member.** A person's spouse or domestic partner, ancestors, siblings (whole or
half) and their spouses, children (natural or adopted), grandchildren, great-grandchildren, and the
spouses of children, grandchildren and great-grandchildren.

**Section 2.4. Examples.** Without limiting Section 2.2, the following are examples of situations
that must be disclosed for a RevolutionVA director or officer:

- the director's employer, client, or a company the director owns or advises is, or is being asked to become, a sponsor, vendor, venue or paid service provider for a RevolutionVA event;
- the director or a Family Member would be paid by the Corporation (for example for design, development, speaking or contracting work), other than reimbursement of actual expenses;
- the director would receive tickets, travel or gifts from a sponsor or vendor that are not insubstantial;
- the director holds a leadership role in another organization that is seeking money or sponsorship from the Corporation.

**Section 2.5. Disinterested Director.** A director who has no Financial Interest in the transaction
or arrangement under consideration.

## Article III — Procedures

**Section 3.1. Duty to Disclose.** In connection with any actual or possible conflict of interest,
an Interested Person must disclose the existence of the Financial Interest and be given the
opportunity to disclose all material facts to the directors considering the proposed transaction or
arrangement. Disclosure should be made as soon as the person becomes aware of it, and in any event
before the Corporation commits to the transaction. Disclosure may be made in writing (including by
email to all directors) and must be recorded under Article IV.

**Section 3.2. Determining Whether a Conflict of Interest Exists.** After disclosure of the
Financial Interest and all material facts, and after any discussion with the Interested Person, the
Interested Person shall leave the meeting (or the portion of a remote meeting) while the
determination of a conflict of interest is discussed and voted upon. The remaining Disinterested
Directors shall decide whether a conflict of interest exists.

**Section 3.3. Procedures for Addressing the Conflict of Interest.**

- (a) An Interested Person may make a presentation at the Board meeting, but after the presentation shall leave the meeting during the discussion of, and the vote on, the transaction or arrangement involving the possible conflict of interest.
- (b) The chair of the meeting shall, if appropriate, appoint a disinterested person or committee to investigate alternatives to the proposed transaction or arrangement (for example, comparable quotes from other vendors).
- (c) After exercising due diligence, the Disinterested Directors shall determine whether the Corporation can obtain, with reasonable efforts, a more advantageous transaction or arrangement from a person or entity that would not give rise to a conflict of interest.
- (d) If a more advantageous transaction or arrangement is not reasonably possible under circumstances not producing a conflict of interest, the Disinterested Directors shall determine by majority vote whether the transaction or arrangement is in the Corporation's best interest, for its own benefit, and fair and reasonable. In conformity with that determination, they shall decide whether to enter into the transaction or arrangement.

**Section 3.4. Small-Board Procedures.** The Board is permitted to have as few as one director
(Bylaws § 2.1) and currently has two. The following apply in addition to Section 3.3 whenever the
ordinary procedure cannot produce an independent decision:

- (a) **Quorum.** An Interested Person may be counted in determining the presence of a quorum (Bylaws § 3.5) but shall not vote on, and shall leave the discussion of, the matter in which the person is interested.
- (b) **Exactly one Disinterested Director.** The Interested Person discloses, abstains and leaves the discussion. The transaction should not be approved by the sole Disinterested Director acting alone unless counsel has confirmed that approval is permitted under the Virginia Nonstock Corporation Act. Before deciding, the Disinterested Director shall (i) obtain and document comparability data (such as competing quotes or market rates), and (ii) where the amount is material or the matter is not routine, obtain the written views of an independent outside advisor under Article VIII. Where approval by a single director is not permitted or is not prudent, the Board shall use one of the options in paragraph (d).
- (c) **No Disinterested Director.** If every director is an Interested Person (for example, a compensation arrangement covering all directors), no director may approve the matter on the Corporation's behalf under this Policy. The Board shall use one of the options in paragraph (d).
- (d) **Options when an independent decision cannot be made.** The Board may: (i) decline or defer the transaction; (ii) elect one or more additional qualified directors under Bylaws §§ 2.1–2.2 who have no Financial Interest, and have them decide the matter; (iii) refer the matter to independent legal counsel or another qualified outside advisor and act only in conformity with that advisor's documented recommendation; or (iv) where permitted by law, seek another form of authorization available under the Virginia Nonstock Corporation Act, as advised by counsel.
- (e) **Written consent.** Action by written consent under Bylaws § 3.4 requires the signature of all directors entitled to vote on the matter. A written consent approving a transaction under this Policy shall state the disclosed Financial Interest, which directors are interested, the comparability data relied on, and that the Interested Person did not vote.
- (f) **Routine expense reimbursement.** Reimbursement of a director's or officer's actual, reasonable, documented out-of-pocket expenses incurred on the Corporation's behalf is permitted by Bylaws § 5.2 and is not compensation under this Policy. Such reimbursements should be approved or reviewed by a director other than the person being reimbursed whenever another director is available, and supporting receipts shall be retained.

**Section 3.5. Violations of the Conflict of Interest Policy.**

- (a) If the Board has reasonable cause to believe a director or officer has failed to disclose an actual or possible conflict of interest, it shall inform the person of the basis for that belief and give the person an opportunity to explain the alleged failure to disclose.
- (b) If, after hearing the response and making further investigation as warranted by the circumstances, the Board determines the person has failed to disclose an actual or possible conflict of interest, it shall take appropriate disciplinary and corrective action, which may include removal as a director or officer (Bylaws §§ 2.2, 10.2), rescission or renegotiation of the transaction where possible, and correction of any excess benefit as required by Section 4958 of the Code.
- (c) A director who is the subject of a possible violation shall not vote on the Board's determination under this Section.

## Article IV — Records of Proceedings

The minutes of the Board and of all committees with Board-delegated powers, and any written consent
under Bylaws § 3.4, shall contain:

- (a) the names of the persons who disclosed or otherwise were found to have a Financial Interest in connection with an actual or possible conflict of interest, the nature of the Financial Interest, any action taken to determine whether a conflict of interest was present, and the Board's or committee's decision as to whether a conflict of interest in fact existed;
- (b) the names of the persons who were present for discussions and votes relating to the transaction or arrangement, the content of the discussion, including any alternatives considered, comparability data obtained and outside advice received, and a record of any votes taken (including abstentions) in connection with the proceedings.

These records are permanent records of the Corporation under Bylaws § 16.1, shall be prepared
before the later of the next meeting of the Board or 60 days after the final action, and shall be
kept in `board/minutes/` (public copy) and with the Corporation's records (signed originals). The
Secretary is responsible for keeping them (Bylaws § 11.5).

## Article V — Compensation

- (a) A voting member of the Board who receives compensation, directly or indirectly, from the Corporation for services is precluded from voting on matters pertaining to that member's compensation.
- (b) A voting member of any committee whose jurisdiction includes compensation matters and who receives compensation, directly or indirectly, from the Corporation for services is precluded from voting on matters pertaining to that member's compensation.
- (c) No voting member of the Board or any committee whose jurisdiction includes compensation matters and who receives compensation, directly or indirectly, from the Corporation, either individually or collectively, is prohibited from providing information to the Board or any committee regarding compensation.
- (d) Any compensation of a director (Bylaws § 5.2) or officer (Bylaws § 11.6) shall be approved under Article III, including Section 3.4 where it applies, and shall be supported by documented comparability data.

## Article VI — Annual Statements

**Section 6.1. Annual Statement.** Each director, principal officer and member of a committee with
Board-delegated powers shall annually sign a statement, in substantially the form of
[Appendix A](#appendix-a--annual-conflict-of-interest-disclosure-and-acknowledgment-statement),
which affirms that the person:

- (a) has received a copy of this Policy;
- (b) has read and understands the Policy;
- (c) has agreed to comply with the Policy; and
- (d) understands the Corporation is charitable and, in order to maintain its federal tax exemption, it must engage primarily in activities which accomplish one or more of its tax-exempt purposes.

**Section 6.2. Timing.** Statements shall be signed (i) by each current director and officer within
30 days after this Policy is adopted, (ii) by each new director or officer upon election, and
(iii) by every director and officer at or before the annual meeting of the Board (Bylaws § 3.1).
A statement shall be updated promptly when a new Financial Interest arises. Electronic signatures
are acceptable.

**Section 6.3. Custody.** Signed statements are kept with the Corporation's records by the
Secretary and are not published. The minutes of the annual meeting shall note that statements were
collected and whether any disclosures were made.

## Article VII — Periodic Reviews

To ensure the Corporation operates in a manner consistent with charitable purposes and does not
engage in activities that could jeopardize its tax-exempt status (Bylaws § 4.2), the Board shall
conduct periodic reviews, at least annually and as part of the annual meeting. The periodic reviews
shall, at a minimum, include the following subjects:

- (a) whether compensation arrangements and benefits are reasonable, based on competent survey information, and the result of arm's-length bargaining;
- (b) whether partnerships, joint ventures, and arrangements with management organizations conform to the Corporation's written policies, are properly recorded, reflect reasonable investment or payments for goods and services, further charitable purposes and do not result in inurement, impermissible private benefit or an excess benefit transaction;
- (c) whether sponsorship, vendor and venue arrangements with entities connected to a director or officer were disclosed and approved under this Policy; and
- (d) whether this Policy remains adequate, including in light of any change in the size of the Board.

## Article VIII — Use of Outside Experts

When conducting the periodic reviews provided for in Article VII, or when deciding a matter under
Section 3.4, the Corporation may, but need not, use outside advisors (such as the Corporation's
legal counsel or accountant). If outside experts are used, their use shall not relieve the Board of
its responsibility for ensuring periodic reviews are conducted. Directors may rely in good faith on
such advisors as provided in Bylaws § 4.1. An outside advisor used under this Policy must not
themselves have a Financial Interest in the matter.

## Article IX — Amendment

This Policy may be amended only by the Board (Bylaws § 7.2(a) reserves amendments of the Conflict of
Interest Policy from the executive committee). The Board shall review this Policy whenever the
Bylaws are restated.
