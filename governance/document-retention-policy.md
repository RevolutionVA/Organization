> **About this document.** This Policy sets how long RevolutionVA keeps its records, how they are
> destroyed when that time is up, and how destruction stops when a legal matter is pending or
> expected. It is the written document retention and destruction policy asked about on
> **Form 990, Part VI, line 14**. It builds on what the Bylaws already require: permanent minutes
> (**§ 16.1**), records kept in written or convertible form (**§ 16.3**), and the specific records
> listed in **§ 16.4** (see [TODO](../TODO.md), item 3). The federal obstruction provision in
> 18 U.S.C. § 1519 applies to every organization, nonprofits included. It is written for a very
> small, all-volunteer board and uses role titles rather than names. Retention periods are
> conservative defaults and should be confirmed by the Corporation's accountant and counsel before
> adoption. Bylaws citations refer to [`governance/bylaws.md`](bylaws.md); the executed original
> Bylaws control in the event of any discrepancy.

## Article I. Purpose and Scope

**Section 1.1. Purpose.** This Policy helps RevolutionVA (the "Corporation") keep the records it
needs for governance, tax, legal and historical purposes; dispose of records it no longer needs in
an orderly way; and never destroy records that are relevant to a lawsuit, audit or government
investigation.

**Section 1.2. Scope.** This Policy applies to all records created or received in the course of
the Corporation's business, in any form, including paper, email, chat messages, shared documents,
spreadsheets, photos, and data held in third-party services (for example ticketing, payment,
email marketing, and file storage platforms). It applies to every director, officer, committee
member and volunteer who handles Corporation records.

## Article II. Responsibilities

**Section 2.1. Secretary.** The **Secretary** is the records custodian for governance records
(Articles, Bylaws, minutes, written consents, policies, signed statements and filings with the
State Corporation Commission) and is responsible for this Policy overall (Bylaws § 11.5).

**Section 2.2. Treasurer.** The **Treasurer**, or the officer the Board designates if there is no
Treasurer, is the records custodian for financial and tax records, in conjunction with the
Corporation's accountant (Bylaws § 11.4).

**Section 2.3. Program Leads.** The volunteer lead for each RevolutionVA program or event (for
example Hampton Roads DevFest) is responsible for event records for that program and for handing
them to the Secretary or Treasurer as appropriate when the lead steps down.

**Section 2.4. Corporation-Controlled Storage.** Corporation records shall be kept in accounts and
storage controlled by the Corporation, not only in a volunteer's personal email, drive or device.
When a director, officer or volunteer leaves a role, they shall transfer any Corporation records
they hold to the appropriate custodian.

## Article III. Retention Schedule

**Section 3.1. Schedule.** Records shall be kept for at least the periods below. "Permanent" means
kept for the life of the Corporation and then handled under Article VII. Periods run from the end
of the fiscal year the record relates to, unless the table says otherwise.

| Record | Retention period |
|---|---|
| **Corporate and governance** | |
| Articles of Incorporation, amendments and restatements | Permanent |
| Bylaws, amendments and restatements | Permanent |
| Board and committee minutes, written consents and resolutions (Bylaws § 16.1) | Permanent |
| Board-adopted policies, including superseded versions | Permanent |
| Signed annual Conflict of Interest and Whistleblower statements | 7 years |
| Record of directors and officers (Bylaws § 16.2) | Permanent |
| State Corporation Commission annual reports and registered agent filings | Permanent |
| Trademark, domain and other intellectual property records | Permanent |
| **Tax and exemption** | |
| IRS determination letter and related correspondence | Permanent |
| Form 1023 or 1023-EZ application and attachments (Bylaws § 16.4(d)) | Permanent |
| Form 990 returns, schedules and amendments | Permanent |
| Other tax filings and IRS or state tax correspondence | Permanent |
| Charitable solicitation registrations or exemptions, if any | 7 years after expiration |
| **Financial** | |
| Year-end financial statements and any audit or review reports | Permanent |
| General ledger and annual accounting records | 7 years |
| Bank and payment processor statements and reconciliations | 7 years |
| Invoices, receipts, expense reimbursements and supporting documentation | 7 years |
| Check registers, deposit records and canceled checks | 7 years |
| Bank account resolutions and signer authorizations (Bylaws § 17.1) | 7 years after superseded |

**Section 3.2. Records Not Listed.** A record not listed above shall be kept for the period that
applies to the most similar category, or for seven (7) years if none applies, unless the Secretary
or Treasurer decides a shorter period is appropriate and records that decision.

**Section 3.3. Personal Information.** Records containing personal information about attendees,
speakers or volunteers should be kept no longer than the schedule requires and should be shared
only with those who need it.

## Article IV. Electronic Records

**Section 4.1. Same Rules for Every Format.** Electronic records, including email, chat messages,
shared documents and data in third-party services, are retained for the same period as paper
records of the same type. What determines the period is the content of a record, not its format.

**Section 4.2. Storage.** Electronic records shall be stored in Corporation-controlled accounts
with access limited to those who need it, organized so the custodian can find them, and kept in a
format that can be converted into written form within a reasonable time (Bylaws § 16.3). Records
held only in a third-party service that may delete data or close an account shall be exported
periodically into Corporation-controlled storage.

**Section 4.3. Email and Chat.** Email and chat messages that are records (for example a signed
sponsorship agreement, a donor acknowledgment, or a Board decision made by email) shall be saved
to the appropriate folder or system for their category. Messages with no business value may be
deleted when no longer needed.

**Section 4.4. Automatic Deletion.** Any automatic deletion or expiration setting on a
Corporation account shall be consistent with this schedule and shall be suspended when a
litigation hold applies.

## Article V. Destruction

**Section 5.1. Annual Review.** Once a year, ideally before the annual meeting (Bylaws § 3.1), the
Secretary and Treasurer shall identify records past their retention period and arrange for their
destruction, unless a litigation hold applies.

**Section 5.2. Method.** Records shall be destroyed in a way that protects confidential and
personal information:

- (a) paper records containing financial, donor or personal information shall be shredded; other paper may be recycled;
- (b) electronic records shall be permanently deleted, including from shared drives, trash folders and backups the Corporation controls; and
- (c) devices and storage media that held Corporation records shall be wiped or physically destroyed before disposal or reuse outside the Corporation.

**Section 5.3. Destruction Log.** The custodian shall keep a simple log of records destroyed,
showing the category, date range, date destroyed and method. The log is kept permanently.

## Article VI. Litigation Hold

**Section 6.1. When a Hold Applies.** When any director or officer learns that the Corporation is
involved in, or reasonably anticipates, a lawsuit, claim, government investigation, subpoena or
audit, they shall immediately notify the President and the Secretary. If the matter involves the
President or the Secretary, they shall notify a director who is not involved instead.

**Section 6.2. Hold Notice.** The President, or a director who is not involved when the President
is involved, shall promptly issue a written litigation hold notice to everyone who may hold relevant
records, identifying the records to be preserved.

**Section 6.3. Effect of a Hold.** A litigation hold suspends all destruction of relevant records
under this Policy, including routine destruction under Article V and automatic deletion under
Section 4.4, regardless of the retention schedule. No one shall destroy, alter, conceal or
falsify any record relevant to a pending or anticipated matter. Doing so may be a federal crime
under 18 U.S.C. § 1519 and may be grounds for removal from a role, office or the Board.

**Section 6.4. Lifting a Hold.** A hold remains in effect until the person who issued it lifts it
in writing, after the matter is fully resolved and, where counsel is involved, after counsel
confirms the hold may be lifted. Records then return to the normal schedule.

## Article VII. Dissolution

If the Corporation dissolves, permanent records shall be transferred to the organization receiving
the Corporation's remaining assets, or to another custodian the Board designates, so they remain
available for any required period.

## Article VIII. Compliance, Amendment and Review

**Section 8.1. Compliance.** Each director and officer shall receive a copy of this Policy upon
taking office. The minutes of the annual meeting shall note that the annual review under Section
5.1 was completed.

**Section 8.2. Amendment and Review.** This Policy may be amended only by the Board. The Board
shall review the retention schedule at least every two years, with input from the Corporation's
accountant, and whenever the Bylaws are restated or the Corporation's activities change (for
example, if it hires employees).
